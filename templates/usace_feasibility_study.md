# Introduction

## Introduction

The introduction should be one to two paragraphs that gives the reader the initial details of the feasibility study that is being conducted.

## USACE Planning Process

Describe the structure of our report and how we plan studies to the reader.

## Study Authority

Include the full text of the resolution or other authority.

## Study Area (Planning Area)

The study area should be adequately described to ensure the reader can identify the geographic siting and boundary and also be informed of major geographic features, resources, and critical infrastructure.

## Background and History

Discuss the background as it relates to the current study.

## Purpose and Need

One or two paragraphs that briefly specify the underlying purpose and need for the study.

## Problems and Opportunities

List out the problem statement(s) and then, as necessary for support, briefly develop and elaborate on the nature, cause, location, and significance of the problem(s) in the report text. Conduct a similar process for the opportunities.

## Objectives and Constraints

The objectives of the study should be listed and then clearly indicate the effect, subject, location, timing, and duration for each objective.

## Study Scope

The scope will guide what the study “will do” and what the study “will not do.”

# Existing and Future Without Project Conditions

This chapter provides both the existing conditions (a baseline), as well as a forecast of the “Future Without Project (FWOP)” conditions, which together provide the basis for plan formulation.

## Period of Analysis

Define the period of analysis.

## General Setting

The General Setting is a very brief, general description of the study area that includes location information and any other study specific features.

## Natural Environment

The natural environment section should include descriptions of wetlands, endangered species, vegetation, and other relevant attributes.

## Physical Environment

The physical environment should include environmental or human related factors and the role that these factors contribute to the study area.

## Built Environment

The built environment describes existing and/or planned federal and local projects.

## Economic Environment

The economic environment is a discussion of the existing economic conditions as wells as assumptions used to generate the FWOP conditions.

# Plan Formulation and Evaluation

The plan formulation section of the Feasibility Report is always the most variable regarding format and content.

## Planning Framework

The beginning and most important part of the plan formulation and evaluation section is the planning framework, as it serves as a guide for the reader to follow and essentially an executive summary of the plan formulation process.

## Assumptions

This section, if necessary, should briefly list or describe the assumptions that were used in the planning process.

## Management Measures

The first task in this section is to briefly define what a management measure is and how measures are used to build alternatives.

## Arrays of Alternatives

The report should first briefly describe the process and formulation strategies that were used to assemble or combine measures into alternative plans.

## Plan Evaluation

After a focused array has been identified and described, each alternative plan is evaluated by projecting and comparing the with project and without project conditions.

# Environmental Effects and Consequences

## Affected Environment (40 CFR 1502.15) and Environmental Consequences (40 CFR 1502.16)

The NEPA regulations in 40 CFR 1502.16 describe the types of impacts and information that should be included in integrated feasibility reports / environmental documents (e.g., incomplete or unavailable information, unavoidable adverse impacts, irreversible and irretrievable commitments of resources, energy requirements, and conservation potential).

## Mitigation, Monitoring, and Adaptive Management

Summarize the mitigation requirements, including compensatory mitigation and modifications made to project features to avoid and minimize effects, with reference to the laws and regulations driving the requirement.

# Plan Comparison and Selection

The Plan Comparison and Selection section must demonstrate to the reader that you have compared all relevant attributes and effects of the alternatives that are needed to make a decision.

## Plan Comparison

The plan comparison section will contrast and judge the alternatives based on performance.

## Identification of the NED or NER Plan

You must indicate which plan is the NED plan, NER plan, or a combination of both.

## Plan Selection

After the plans have been compared using the P&G criteria, four accounts, environmental consequences, and other relevant criteria in Section 5.1, and the NED, NER, or combined plan has been identified, the report must indicate which plan was selected and why.

## Deviations from the NED or NER Plan

This section is only necessary if a plan other than the NED plan or NER plan, including an LPP, was selected (Section 5.3).

# The Recommended Plan

Section 6 should contain sufficient information for the reader to understand the Recommended Plan from a physical and construction standpoint, in terms of the project’s up to date (current Fiscal Year (FY)) cost and benefits (both monetary and non-monetary), associated risks, and the responsibilities of the federal government and sponsor before, during, and after project construction.

## Plan Accomplishments

This section should fully describe the monetary and nonmonetary benefits of the project.

## Plan Components

Include a detailed description of all project features, along with any relevant pictures and design drawings.

## Cost Estimate

List the “Project First Cost” at the current fiscal year price level, as well as a breakdown, at a minimum, of Pre-construction Engineering and Design (PED), mitigation costs, Lands, Easements, Rights-of-Way, Relocations, and Disposal (LERRDs), and construction.

## Lands, Easements, Rights-of-Way, Relocations, and Disposal

Describe the LERRDs needed for the project as well as the estimated cost of LERRDs.

## Operations, Maintenance, Repair, Replacement and Rehabilitation (OMRR&R)

Describe the OMRR&R requirements for the project, as well as the estimated cost of those activities.

## Project Risks

Describe any risks related to implementation of the project and/or any elements of the project performance as described in the previous sections, as well as how those risks are to be or have been managed.

## Cost Sharing

List both the percentage and the amount of the federal and non-federal share of the “Project First Cost” in a tabular format.

## Design and Construction

Include the estimated design and construction schedule as well as any applicable timing constraints that may affect the schedule (e.g., dredging windows, LERRD acquisition).

## Environmental Commitments

Include a concise description of all commitments made to comply with project requirements.

## Project-Specific Considerations

Any other items that are relevant for project implementation, that are not covered by the previous sections, should be documented in this subsection.

## Environmental Operating Principles (EOP)

Include a brief discussion of how the Recommended Plan meets the USACE Environmental Operating Principles.

## Views of the Non-Federal Sponsor

Provide a concise narrative from the nonfederal sponsor regarding support and implementation of the Recommended Plan as well as any agency that has implementation responsibilities (Reference ER 1105-2-100, Exhibit G-7).

# Environmental Compliance

## Environmental Compliance Table

List all relevant environmental laws, regulations, and executive orders (EOs) with a brief statement summarizing how the project will comply with the requirements.

## Public Involvement

Briefly summarize the scoping process (e.g., number, location, and dates of meetings and attendance) and the nature and level of intensity of public and agency concerns.

# District Engineer Recommendation

For content, see ER 1105-2-100, Appendix G, Section G-9.i.

# List of Preparers

## List of Preparers

Briefly list the names and qualifications of all individuals, including contractors, involved in preparation of the document and significant supporting information.
