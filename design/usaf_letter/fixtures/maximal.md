Thank you for asking about the format and purpose of the personal letter. The personal letter conveys official information in a personal manner to a limited number of people, which is what makes it the right form for a reply to an inquiry, a welcome, a note of appreciation, or a letter of condolence.

The letter is set in 12-point Times New Roman on approved organizational letterhead. Its first page begins 1.75 inches from the top; the left, right, and bottom margins are one inch, and so is the top margin of every page after the first.

The heading elements carry the offsets the handbook gives them. The date sits flush with the right margin. Your own rank, name, duty title, and mailing address begin on the second line below it, and the receiver's on the third line below your last line. The salutation follows on the second line below the receiver's address and takes no punctuation after the last name.

The body's paragraphs are indented half an inch and take no number or letter. One blank line separates them. Lines that stand as written — a roster of names, an address — go in a block quote, typeset flush with the margin:

> FIRST M. LAST, Maj, USAF\
> SECOND N. LAST, Capt, USAF

The complimentary close sits on the second line below the last line of text, and the signature block five lines below the close: the four blank lines between them are where the letter is signed. A personal letter carries no duty title under the signature, the heading having given it already.

A letter of three or more pages is numbered from its second page; the first page is never numbered. The attachment element opens on the third line below the signature block and the courtesy copy element on the second line below the attachment element.
