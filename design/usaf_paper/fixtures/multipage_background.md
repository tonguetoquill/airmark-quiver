1. This paper gives the background of the base's move from a local help desk to the enterprise service desk, the effects the move has had on the squadron's network operations, and the decisions that remain before the transition closes. It covers the history of the move, where it stands today, and what it asks of the wing.

2. The base has run its own help desk since the network was installed, staffed by the communications squadron and answering every user on the installation. Each tenant unit has come to rely on that desk for problems the enterprise desk does not take, from local printers to mission systems that sit outside the enterprise network.

   1. The local desk answered about 1,400 calls a month over the last year, and closed about four in five of them on the first call without sending a technician to the user's desk.
   2. The enterprise desk took over account and email problems on 1 March 2026, which cut the local desk's volume by about a third in the first quarter after the change.

      1. Calls about local printers and shared drives did not move, because the enterprise desk does not hold the permissions to fix them and returns each one to the squadron as a new ticket.
      2. Users report waiting longer on the calls that bounce between the two desks, and the squadron has no way to see a ticket once it leaves the local queue.

         1. The wing's last user survey put the median wait on a returned ticket at three duty days, against one duty day for a ticket the local desk opened itself.

   3. The squadron moved four technicians from the desk to the network operations section after the first quarter, where the workload had grown with the new wireless network.

3. The move has changed what the squadron owns. The enterprise desk owns the user's first contact, and the squadron owns the systems the enterprise desk cannot reach, but no agreement says so in writing, and each new system the wing fields raises the question again.

   1. The draft service agreement at the attachment names the systems the squadron keeps, and sets a time within which the enterprise desk passes a ticket back.
   2. The draft has not been coordinated with the tenant units, several of which run systems the agreement does not list.

4. Three decisions remain before the transition closes on 30 September 2027: whether the squadron keeps a staffed local desk at all, which tenant systems the agreement covers, and who pays for the ticketing link between the two desks.

   1. Keeping a local desk of two technicians answers the returned tickets within a duty day, at the cost of the positions the network section now fills.
   2. Closing the desk entirely puts every call through the enterprise desk, and leaves the tenant systems without a first contact until the agreement covers them.

5. The transition has moved most of the routine work off the base without a loss in service, but the work that stays local has no owner on paper. A signed agreement that lists the systems each desk owns would settle the question for each new system, and would let the wing decide the size of the local desk on the calls that remain rather than on the calls that moved.

6. This paper is background for the wing commander's decision at the November staff meeting. The squadron will brief the three options at that meeting, with the cost of each, and with the tenant units' comments on the draft agreement.

7. The squadron keeps the call data behind each figure in this paper, and can break it out by tenant unit on request.
