# Project Authorization and History

## Project Authorization

The Flood Control Act of 1938 authorizes Detroit Dam, as modified by the Flood Control Act of 1950.

## Project History

Detroit Dam has regulated the North Santiam River since 1953. It provides flood risk management, hydropower, irrigation, and fish and wildlife benefits to the Willamette Valley.

### Regulating outlets

The four regulating outlet (RO) gates pass flow when the turbines are offline.

#### Gate condition

The 2020 condition assessment rates the RO gate seals as unsatisfactory.

##### Seal wear.

Seal leakage exceeds the manufacturer's limit at gates 2 and 3.

###### Embedded metals.

The embedded guides show pitting below the 1,450-foot elevation.

# Proposed Scope of Work

The work replaces the RO gate seals and rehabilitates the embedded metals.

1. Dewater the RO conduits behind the emergency gates.
   1. Install the bulkheads.
   2. Pump the conduits dry.
2. Replace the gate seals.
3. Return the gates to service.

| Gate | Seal condition | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RO 1 | Satisfactory | Inspect |
| RO 2 | Unsatisfactory | Replace |
| RO 3 | Unsatisfactory | Replace |
| RO 4 | Satisfactory | Inspect |

**Note.** Gate numbers run from the left abutment.

# Justification for Maintenance

Seal leakage limits the gates' ability to hold the pool for temperature operations.

## Hydrodynamic Loads

The hydrodynamic pressure on the gates under the Operational Basis Earthquake (OBE) follows the Westergaard equation:

$$ p = 0.875 W a_c \sqrt{H y} $$

**Where:**

- $p$ = hydrodynamic pressure
- $W$ = unit weight of water
- $a_c$ = maximum base acceleration of the dam
- $H$ = reservoir height
- $y$ = depth below the pool surface
